Systems Programming Roadmap

One flagship project (stash, a quick way to send files, text, links, photos and video between your Linux, Windows, Android and iPhone devices, on your home network or over the internet) that every language plugs into. Each project below has a scope, what you learn, milestone checklists, and a clear "done when". Tick boxes as you go; progress is saved in your browser.

How each device shares

One always-on machine runs the hub. Everything talks to it over HTTP, so a browser is enough to start. Sharing and sync must work both on your home network and over the internet. There are no native phone apps to write: Swift and Kotlin are not on the language list, and a web app plus the iPhone's Shortcuts app covers sharing.

    Suggested pacing

    Estimates assume about 6 to 8 focused hours a week alongside work and law school. One "weekend" is roughly one 4 to 6 hour block. They are guesses, so adjust as you learn how fast you move. You can share between all four devices on your home network at the end of phase 0, and over the internet in phase 2. Every later phase makes that nicer or teaches something deeper.

    How the C and C++ projects differ

    The two languages get different kinds of work, so nothing repeats. C projects are small, file-and-bytes programs where you do everything by hand. C++ projects are long-running, interactive programs where abstraction and RAII are the point.

    CC++
    ProjectsDuplicate file finder, stash-fsck (storage checker), libstash (C ABI client library), the WASM location-stripper plugin, mDNS responder, eBPF kernel programsThe first LAN file server, terminal UI, live updates in the TUI, io_uring edge proxy
    Kind of programMostly small tools and libraries working on bytesLong-running, event-driven programs that hold many connections
    What it trainsRaw bytes, syscalls, manual memory, defensive parsing, fuzzingRAII, move semantics, classes and templates, variants, event loops
    Tooling focusValgrind, ASan, libFuzzer or AFL++, MakefileCMake, clang-tidy, sanitizers, unit-test framework
